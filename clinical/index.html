After he left home, Tria was treated first for exhaustion, injuries and prolonged sleep loss. During later appointments he described hearing a familiar voice when nobody was present, feeling watched, and sometimes being unable to trust whether a figure or message was real.

After continued assessment, he was diagnosed with schizophrenia. The diagnosis did not mean he was violent, broken, or responsible for what had happened to him. With stable housing, antipsychotic medication, sleep and regular appointments, the voices became less frequent and he learned ways to test what he was experiencing.

"A symptom can feel completely real. That does not make the person dishonest."
